Verity Compliance — Solution Review

How the solution answers the compliance analyst's pain points

An as-built review of the tenant compliance application (and the central intelligence platform that feeds it), mapped to the six pain points of a working compliance analyst — with the exact screens and a worked example for each.

Version 3.1 — as-built Tenant app :5174 Intel platform :5173 Analyst persona Ngozi, mid-size Nigerian bank

The solution in one model

Every screen is a view onto one chain. The analyst never types a claim of compliance; the system reads the evidence trail and derives the answer.

Instrument → Obligation → Requirement → Control → Test → Evidence (what the (one duty per (one duty per (what we (does it (proof) regulator line) line) do about it) work?) published) │ ▼ Finding ← Failure / Gap │ ▼ Risk (inherent → residual, live)

Invariant 1 — nothing counts until it is accepted

A control test moves nothing (residual risk, obligation compliance, control effectiveness, the score) until it is Accepted at review by someone other than the author. A recorded test sits Pending.

Invariant 2 — compliance state is derived, never stored

No one can assert "we are compliant." State is read from requirement coverage and accepted tests: Compliant / Partial / Non-compliant / No control.

1Finding new regulations manually

The pain

Ngozi checks 12 regulator websites a week; the 30-day compliance window is often half gone before action starts because she learned of a circular by forwarded email.

What the solution does

The central platform runs a Horizon Scanner across the configured Nigerian regulators every 15 minutes. New documents are downloaded, OCR'd (PDFBox, Tesseract fallback for scans), and passed to the AI classifier, which returns structured fields (area of focus, risk rating, licence types, duties, sanctions) — not free text. Toolkit instruments seed straight to the register; scraped/AI instruments land in the tenant Review Inbox, where a human must Edit & Save before anything reaches the register. The tenant then sees what applies to it through subscriptions and the confirmed universe in one place.

Screens

Compliance Universe
/universe

Risk KPIs, Top Regulators, Nature mix, Areas of Focus. Every card/chip drills into a filtered instrument table; a row opens a drawer with the AI summary and its obligations.

Review Inbox
/review

The queue of newly arrived documents awaiting a human decision; a row expands to an enriched, read-only obligation summary (no N+1).

Review & Edit
/review/:reviewId

Inline edit of title, verbatim text, plain-English and risk. Save is the only path from document to register.

Uploads
/uploads

Manual document intake into the same pipeline.

Example

CBN publishes a circular at 02:14. By morning it appears in Review Inbox with a plain-English summary and a risk rating. Ngozi opens the review, corrects one duty's wording to match the bank's operation, sets the risk rating, and saves. The instrument now appears under Compliance Universe → Regulators → Central Bank of Nigeria, and each duty becomes a row in the Obligations register. Nothing was typed from scratch and no website was visited.

Design principle — human in the loop

The AI proposes, the analyst disposes. A false claim can never enter the register un-reviewed.

2350+ obligations in an unstructured spreadsheet

The pain

A 1,200-row sheet where columns mean different things to different people, no ownership, no audit trail, and no reliable answer to "show me all High-risk AML obligations with no control."

What the solution does

The register is one row per enforceable duty (not per document) with typed fields: verbatim description vs interpreted plain_english_statement, area of focus, inherent risk, act/regulation, section and recurring deadline. Two capabilities turn the list into a working register:

  • Requirements — each obligation carries one-or-more requirements, the finest unit of duty, with a derived state. Requirements are the single place the duty text lives and the unit that controls attach to.
  • One gap definition, everywhere — an obligation is a gap when at least one of its requirements has no linked control. That single definition drives the register's No Control KPI/filter, the Task Center's gap rows, and the finding close gate.

Obligation detail

Header (act, regulator, area, inherent risk) with tabs; the Requirements tab is the default and is where the duty text lives — one requirement per line with its derived state, its covering controls, and coverage effective/total.

Requirement detail

Acts

Example (grounded in the seeded tenant)

The register holds 1,407 obligations; risk bands read High 1,197 · Moderate 205 · Critical 4 · Low 1. Opening obligation "General Principles" (Corporate Governance, High) shows its requirement lines; a requirement with no control reads No control and counts toward the register's No Control KPI. Filtering by Area of Focus = AML/CFT/CPF narrows to 332 obligations and the KPI strip updates — the exact question is now three clicks, not a spreadsheet formula.

Known defect surfaced by this review

On the seeded tenant, obligations.control_owner reads a risk word ("High") for 217 Corporate Governance obligations (e.g. obligation 122 "General Principles" → owner High) because the offline toolkit exporter resolved the owner column by a hard-coded index that mis-aligned on that sheet. The correct actor is present in the neighbouring responsibility text. This is a seed-data bug, not a model bug (owner is free text, not an FK). Repaired at source — see Status & gaps.

3187 return deadlines with no safety net

The pain

Deadlines tracked in a shared calendar with no preparation workflow; a regulator changes a date and the calendar is never updated; the bank pays the fine even though the work was done.

What the solution does

Returns are a first-class module with a five-stage workflow — Not Started → Data Gathering → Draft → Review → Completed — and a decision gate at each review. Deadlines come from a due-date rule (an offset from period end, a fixed date, or event-driven), not a copied field, so a corrected schedule regenerates untouched periods. A policy-driven escalation engine runs on a 5-minute tick: approaching, reminder, due-today and overdue rungs climb a ladder (responsible person → manager → CCO), each emailing the right people with the owner Cc'd. Filing can be done through a no-login public link, and the CCO's acceptance completes the period and rolls the schedule forward. Once accepted, a proof of filing receipt can be attached.

Public filing page

Example (grounded)

Return "Basel III Capital Adequacy Returns" is MONTHLY, its rule derived from the platform's own wording. A filing is created with the successor projected (the next period appears only when the current one is accepted). As the due date nears, the engine fires the approaching email with a fresh /return-upload/{token} to the responsible person (owner Cc'd); if they do not act, the overdue rung escalates to their manager, then the CCO. The responsible person files through the link; the analyst accepts the data (→ Draft); the CCO accepts the report (→ Completed), the schedule rolls forward, and a proof of filing receipt can be uploaded and appears under Evidence Vault → Returns.

Design principle — the deadline is the system's, not a memory

Every rung is policy-driven and editable in Settings, and every send is logged.

4Producing evidence for examiners takes days

The pain

An examiner asks for a control test from 8 months ago; two days vanish searching email, SharePoint and WhatsApp. The bank cannot prove it did the right thing — even when it did.

What the solution does

Every artifact — a test result, a remediation file, a filing, a receipt — is a stored evidence_files row bound to the thing it proves (sourceType/sourceId), downloadable through an authenticated endpoint. Evidence has one home and every item deep-links back to its subject. An obligation's Evidence tab is a rollup of its own evidence plus its controls' test evidence, its findings' remediation, and its returns' filing/receipt evidence. Separately, every state change writes an append-only, hash-chained audit event, so the trail is tamper-evident.

Screens

Category KPI chips, search + source-type filter, and a 5-column table (File · Category · Linked to · Uploaded by · Uploaded) with deep links and a download drawer.

Obligation → Evidence tab
/obligations/:id

The rollup described above — test evidence via controls, remediation via findings, receipts via returns.

Audit Trail
/audit

The chained event log for any subject.

Proof of filing
/returns/:returnId

Attach the filing receipt on the return detail after acceptance.

Example

An examiner names a control test. Ngozi opens Evidence Vault, filters to Control tests, finds the entry labelled "Control test · CTL-0001 Identity Verification Check", and downloads the file; the Linked to column also jumps straight to the control. If they instead ask about an obligation, its Evidence tab shows the test evidence via the control, the remediation via the finding, and the filing receipt via the return — one screen, no email search.

5Chasing colleagues for tests and attestations

The pain

"Did you test that control?" lives in WhatsApp and email; the work is invisible, untracked, and disappears into chat history.

What the solution does

Work is assigned and chased by the system, not by Ngozi:

  • Control testing separates the Tester (who runs the test) from the owner (who manages the control). The tester is emailed a deterministic, no-login /control-test/{token} link; the test cannot be submitted without evidence; it lands as Pending and only becomes effective when Accepted at review (analysts may self-review; CCO/reviewer roles stay maker–checker). Tests recorded in-app or by a runbook are auto-accepted.
  • Findings are raised automatically from failed tests (and manually for gaps), remediated by the owner through a /remediate/{token} link, and closed under a review gate with an SLA.
  • Escalation chases every due test, finding and filing up the manager chain, emailing concise, per-event messages.
  • Task Center aggregates all of it into one derived queue.

Example

A monthly control test comes due. The engine emails the tester (owner Cc'd) with a link; the tester submits a PASS with a PDF, which cannot go through without the file. The test sits Pending; the analyst opens the review page, sees the control, its basis and the evidence, and Accepts. Only now does the obligation's state move and the control's effectiveness update. If the tester goes quiet, the overdue rung escalates to their manager, then the CCO — all logged, none of it in WhatsApp.

6Board pack takes 4 days from stale data

The pain

3–4 days pulling spreadsheets into PowerPoint; by presentation the data is two weeks old; a follow-up question needs a manual lookup.

What the solution does

Metrics are derived from the live evidence trail, not typed in, and snapshotted daily so trends exist without manual assembly. A compliance score is a weighted mean of coverage, control effectiveness, returns-on-time and findings-within-SLA — with the weights and the band thresholds configurable per firm. Every KPI is clickable to the underlying rows.

7Runbooks — executable procedures with built-in evidence

A runbook turns "how we do this" from a PDF in a shared drive into a versioned, executable procedure that produces its own evidence as it is worked — the missing link between an obligation and the proof it was discharged.

The pain

The procedure for a quarterly access review lives in a Word file and in the analyst's head. When it is run, the outputs (account exports, exception lists, sign-offs) scatter across email and drives, nobody can prove which version was followed, and the evidence for the linked control is reconstructed by hand after the fact.

What the solution does

Runbooks are a first-class, executable object. A runbook is versioned (DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → PUBLISHED, with a SHA-256 content hash at publish and a diff between versions) and linted before it can be submitted (can't publish a procedure with no steps, no escalation on a HIGH/CRITICAL runbook, no framework clause, or a secret in a command). Each version is a tree of sections → steps, each step carrying an instruction, an optional command block, an expected result, an assigned role, blocking/dual-control flags, and mandatory evidence requirements. Runbooks map to obligations, controls and framework clauses.

Executing a runbook (an execution) walks a state machine — NOT_STARTED → PREFLIGHT → IN_PROGRESS → PENDING_SIGNOFF → COMPLETED_PASS (with PAUSED / ESCALATED / ROLLING_BACK branches) — with a pre-flight acknowledgement, enforced step order, blocking steps that cannot be skipped, dual control (a second operator re-authenticates), automatic escalation up the manager chain on an SLA breach, and rollback steps that become tracked steps when a failure triggers them. Evidence is captured per step, hashed server-side, and stored. On final CCO sign-off the runbook writes a control test result back to each linked control — so the runbook closure is the test that drives obligation compliance and residual risk. The closure produces a downloadable evidence pack: a ZIP of every file plus a manifest.json, a manifest.sha256, and a PDF report — re-hashing every file on assembly.

Design principle — the procedure proves itself

The analyst does not assemble evidence afterwards; the runbook captures it as each step is worked, and the closed runbook writes the control-test result back into the compliance graph. One artifact, from procedure to proof.

The two pain points the original list missed

"What is actually my risk?"

Each obligation/risk carries an inherent 5×5 band; the residual is derived live from the obligation's controls' accepted test results (the analyst chooses how far effectiveness lowers the likelihood), so residual risk updates the moment a test is accepted and reverts when a remediation is proven.

Screens: the obligation detail risk block, the Rendition & Coverage risk profile / heatmap, and the risk detail view.

"What do I do today?"

The Task Center is the single answer. It is derived from the returns/controls/findings/obligations repositories, scoped to the caller, and always reflects the true state; the sidebar badge shows the org-wide backlog.

Screen: /tasks.

Cross-cutting capabilities that make it analyst-friendly

Review gates are configurable

Settings → Compliance toggles require a review for control tests and require a review for findings (both default on). With a gate off, a recorded test is auto-accepted — the firm chooses its own assurance level.

Maker–checker with a deliberate exception

CCO / TENANT_ADMIN / dedicated reviewer roles enforce separation of duties; an ANALYST may self-review (analysts do everything). The recorder can accept their own work; a reviewer cannot rubber-stamp someone else's two roles at once.

One vocabulary

"Gap" has exactly one meaning everywhere (a requirement with no control). Emails are concise and per-event (Changes requested: control test ABACA001). Band words are unified across the toolkit and the matrix.

Everything deep-links

A task → the exact test/finding/filing. A KPI → the exact rows. Evidence → its subject. A URL encodes the view.

Nothing is asserted

Compliance state, effectiveness, residual risk and the score are all read from accepted evidence.

Honest status and open items

Owner seed defect — open, repaired at source, not yet propagated

The 217 Corporate-Governance obligations with a risk word as owner are fixed in the committed toolkit JSON (tools/toolkit-enrichment/fix_owner_risk_words.py); the central platform re-seeds clean (0 such rows). The tenant still carries the old values until it is re-seeded, because the seed import de-dupes rather than updates. A tenant re-seed is required to clear it.

Runbooks

The core slice (library, builder, execution console, evidence pack with a verifiable hash, approvals, coverage matrix, IT_OPS role) ships; the calendar, review queue, analytics, Settings tab and MD/PDF export are follow-ups. A worked sample run of RB-ACC-001 against the LDAP test bed produced a full evidence pack — see tab 7 · Runbooks. The seeded templates no longer ship Windows PowerShell command examples.

Examiner portal

Read-only external login is still Planned; today examiners are served from the Evidence Vault and Audit Trail.

Live activity

The freshly seeded tenant has obligations, controls (2,137) and returns (137) but no test or finding activity yet, so the test/finding walkthroughs describe what the analyst will see once they work the queue; the register/returns/evidence examples are grounded in seeded data.
